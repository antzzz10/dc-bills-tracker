- **MATERIAL — “Any not enacted by then die” is too absolute.** A bill presented to the President before Congress ends can still be signed afterward within the constitutional ten-day period, excluding Sundays. [CRS explicitly addresses this exception](https://www.congress.gov/crs_external_products/IN/PDF/IN11569/IN11569.2.pdf). Also, one of the six pending resolutions is [H.J.Res. 31, a proposed constitutional amendment](/Users/andriathomas/Projects/dc-bills-tracker/src/data/bills.json:2433): Congress proposes it to the states rather than enacting it as law. If proposed before Congress ends, ratification need not finish then. Simple and concurrent resolutions likewise are agreed to rather than enacted, although neither type is in this pending set. [Senate explanation](https://www.senate.gov/legislative/common/briefing/leg_laws_acts.htm). **Smallest fix:** “Any still awaiting congressional approval at final adjournment would need to start over next Congress.”

- **NOTE — “Pending” matches the site, but has a narrower meaning than readers may expect.** Here it means reviewed, unarchived and **not yet past either chamber**; it excludes measures that cleared one chamber but still await further action. [Definition](/Users/andriathomas/Projects/dc-bills-tracker/src/lib/measureStatus.js:27). “Have not cleared either chamber” makes that distinction explicit.

- **NOTE — The date is correct.** The 119th Congress’s term ends **at noon January 3, 2027**, under [20th Amendment §1](https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/HMAN-118/html/HMAN-118-pg113.htm). Unfinished legislative business dies at the Congress’s **final sine die adjournment**, which can precede that deadline; an adjournment between sessions of the same Congress does not kill it. [CRS procedure](https://www.congress.gov/crs_external_products/R/PDF/R42977/R42977.13.pdf). Riders are provisions: a future Congress could propose the language again in another spending bill or amendment, without introducing a standalone rider bill.
